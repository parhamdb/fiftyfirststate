# Failed Democratic Transitions vs. Instant Statehood: Raw Data

## 1. Failed Democratic Transitions After Regime Change

### Iraq After 2003

**Casualties:**
- Iraq Body Count project: 185,194-208,167 documented violent civilian deaths through June 2020
- Population-based studies range from 151,000 violent deaths (as of June 2006) to over 1,000,000 excess deaths
- 4,500+ US service members killed; 32,000+ wounded
- Brown University Costs of War Project: over 200,000 total deaths

**Cost:**
- Brown University estimate: $1.922 trillion (long-term, including veterans care, interest on debt)
- Earlier Brown estimate: $2.2 trillion including veterans care through 2053
- Initial US government estimate before the war: $50-60 billion
- Combined Iraq/Syria war zone cost since 9/11: $2.1 trillion

**Constitution Timeline:**
- Provisional Iraqi Constitution (TAL) signed: March 8, 2004 (drafted December 2003-March 2004)
- Permanent constitution approved by referendum: October 15, 2005
- Total time from invasion to permanent constitution: 2.5 years
- Iraq has had two constitutions total: the 1925 monarchical constitution and the 2005 constitution

**Institutional Failures:**
- CPA Order 1 (May 16, 2003): De-Baathification removed all Ba'ath Party-affiliated public sector employees
- CPA Order 2 (May 23, 2003): Dissolved Iraqi security forces -- 385,000 armed forces, 285,000 Interior Ministry (police), 50,000 presidential security units
- Approximately 400,000 soldiers made unemployed overnight
- This created a massive pool of armed, hostile, unemployed men who fueled the insurgency
- Sectarian civil war erupted between Shia majority and Sunni minority
- ISIS reconstituted after the Shia-dominated government failed to integrate Sunni fighters who had fought jihadists

**Elections:**
- January 30, 2005: First post-invasion election (to draft constitution)
- December 2005: Election for permanent legislature
- 2010, 2014, 2018, 2021: Subsequent elections, each contested or controversial
- 20+ years later, democratic institutions remain fragile

**Economic Recovery:**
- Oil and electricity did not return to pre-invasion levels until summer 2008 (5 years)
- Oil exports returned to pre-war levels by mid-2009 (6 years)
- Foreign direct investment remains minimal: less than 0.4% of GDP as of 2022
- 20 years after invasion, Iraq still struggles to attract FDI due to corruption, instability, bloated public sector

### Libya After 2011

**Casualties:**
- 2011 civil war alone: 15,000-30,000 killed (various estimates)
- Academic study: 21,490 killed, 19,700 injured, 435,000 displaced (Feb 2011-Feb 2012)
- Libyan government estimate (Jan 2013): 4,700 rebel fighters and similar number of loyalist soldiers killed
- 1,142-2,515 civilians likely killed in 212 airstrike incidents during 2011 war
- Post-2011 continued violence: hundreds more killed in ongoing factional fighting

**Constitutional Status:**
- An August 2011 interim constitutional declaration (issued by unelected National Transitional Council) still serves as the governing document
- Constitutional Drafting Assembly elected in 2014
- Draft constitution approved by CDA in 2017
- House of Representatives never held a referendum on the draft
- As of 2025: Libya STILL has no permanent constitution -- 14 years and counting
- Libya has not had an adopted constitution since 1969

**State Collapse:**
- Since 2014: country split between rival administrations (east and west)
- Numerous militias control territory; state of lawlessness
- Elections scheduled for December 2021 were postponed indefinitely
- 2020: 2.5 million people affected by the crisis, 1.3 million needing humanitarian assistance
- Libyans are poorer and face more political repression than under Gaddafi by many measures
- A succession of internationally supported "transitions" have all failed

---

### Egypt After 2011

**Timeline:**
- Feb 11, 2011: Mubarak resigns; military (SCAF) takes control
- Nov 2011-Jan 2012: Legislative elections; Muslim Brotherhood wins majority
- June 2012: Mohamed Morsi wins presidency with 51.73% (first democratically elected president)
- Dec 2012: Morsi pushes through constitution via controversial referendum (64% approval)
- July 3, 2013: Military coup led by General Abdel Fattah el-Sisi overthrows Morsi
- Total time from revolution to military coup: 2 years, 5 months

**Casualties:**
- Security forces killed well over 2,000 protesters between 2011-2014
- Rabaa Massacre (August 14, 2013): Egyptian government says 595 protesters and 43 police killed
- Human Rights Watch documented at least 817 killed at Rabaa alone; likely over 1,000
- Muslim Brotherhood claims approximately 2,600 killed at Rabaa
- Deadliest single day in Egypt since the 2011 revolution

**Constitutions:**
- 1971 constitution suspended by military after Mubarak fell
- 2012 constitution pushed through by Morsi (controversial, Islamist-leaning)
- 2014 constitution adopted after coup under military-backed government
- Three constitutions in three years -- none produced stability

**Democratic Failure:**
- 2014: Sisi wins presidential election in landslide criticized as lacking democratic standards
- Demands of the revolution (bread, freedom, dignity, democracy) remain unmet
- Military became more entrenched in politics than before the revolution
- "Maximal repression of revolutionary practices" under Sisi

### Afghanistan After 2001

**Cost:**
- Pentagon estimate: $837 billion direct military costs
- Special Inspector General (SIGAR): $849.7 billion cumulative US spending
- Biden administration estimate: over $2 trillion total
- Breakdown of $2.3 trillion: $800B+ military operations, $88B training Afghan forces, $145B reconstruction, $530B interest on borrowing, $296B veteran care
- $145 billion reconstruction effort marked by "waste, fraud and abuse"
- $72.7 billion in military aid to Afghan forces (2001-2020)

**Casualties:**
- Over 200,000 people killed in 20 years
- 70,000 of those were civilians
- Afghanistan already had the world's largest refugee population before 2001

**Constitution:**
- 2001 Bonn Conference reinstated 1964 constitution as interim law
- 2004 constitution adopted but included few provisions for democratic decision-making
- Many democratic provisions that were included were never implemented
- Highly centralized, Kabul-centric institutions undermined citizen trust

**Institutional Failures:**
- Corruption endemic at all levels, fueled by vast foreign aid
- Parliament plagued by corruption, ethnic divisions, inefficiency, outdated laws
- Public trust in government fell dramatically
- Power concentrated in the executive
- Afghan security forces collapsed in days when US withdrew
- Republic ended August 15, 2021 -- president fled by helicopter
- 20 years of institution-building erased in weeks

## 2. The Complexity of Building Democracy from Scratch

### How Long Does It Take?

**Constitution Writing:**
- Iraq: 2.5 years from invasion to permanent constitution (2003-2005)
- Libya: 14+ years and still no permanent constitution
- Egypt: Three constitutions in three years (2012, 2013 suspended, 2014), none produced stability
- Afghanistan: Constitution adopted in 2004, collapsed by 2021

**Democratic Consolidation Research:**
- Academic standard: democracy considered "consolidated" after free, fair elections for 20+ years
- Key indicator: two consecutive peaceful turnovers of power
- Transitional regimes may remain unstable for decades
- A transition does not always lead to consolidation (reverse waves of democratization are common)

**How Many Elections Before Stability?**
- Iraq: 5+ national elections since 2005, still not stable
- Afghanistan: Multiple elections over 20 years, all contested, country collapsed
- Egypt: Elections in 2011-2012, ended in military coup by 2013
- Libya: Elections in 2012 and 2014, then rival governments; 2021 elections postponed indefinitely

**Judiciary, Police, Military Reform:**
- Iraq: CPA reported 90% of courthouses reopened by September 2003, but 400,000 security personnel were simultaneously disbanded, fueling insurgency
- Iraq Supreme Judicial Council established 2003, but judiciary remained weak and politically influenced
- Afghanistan: 20 years of police and military training; forces collapsed within days of US withdrawal
- Cost of training Afghan security forces alone: $88 billion

**Currency Stabilization:**
- Iraq: New Iraqi dinar introduced October 2003; currency remained volatile for years
- Afghanistan: New afghani introduced in 2002; economy remained aid-dependent throughout

**Foreign Investment Timeline:**
- Iraq: Oil/electricity did not return to pre-war levels for 5-6 years
- Iraq FDI still less than 0.4% of GDP two decades later
- Foreign investors consistently cite corruption, instability, and lack of rule of law as barriers

## 3. What You Get Instantly with US Statehood

### Federal Institutions That Immediately Apply

Upon admission to the Union, the "Equal Footing Doctrine" guarantees a new state receives identical rights, protections, and federal services as all existing states. These include:

**Law Enforcement & Justice:**
- FBI jurisdiction and field offices
- US Marshals Service
- Federal court system (District Court, Court of Appeals, access to Supreme Court)
- Federal public defenders
- DEA, ATF, Secret Service protection

**Financial & Economic:**
- FDIC-insured banking (deposits insured up to $250,000)
- Federal Reserve banking system
- SEC securities regulation
- IRS tax system
- US currency (world's reserve currency)
- SBA small business loans
- Federal contracting opportunities

**Healthcare & Social Safety Net:**
- Social Security (retirement, disability, survivors benefits)
- Medicare (65+ healthcare)
- Medicaid (expanded, full federal matching)
- Supplemental Security Income (SSI)
- SNAP (food assistance, full federal program)
- Veterans Affairs healthcare and benefits

**Infrastructure:**
- Federal Highway Administration funding
- FAA air traffic control and airport regulation
- FRA railroad safety
- Army Corps of Engineers
- US Postal Service

**Safety & Emergency:**
- FEMA disaster response and recovery
- National Guard integration
- Coast Guard (if applicable)
- NOAA weather services
- CDC disease surveillance

**Regulation & Protection:**
- FDA food and drug safety
- EPA environmental protection
- OSHA workplace safety
- FCC communications regulation
- Consumer Financial Protection Bureau

**Education:**
- Federal student loans and Pell Grants
- Department of Education funding
- Title I school funding
- Head Start programs

**Political Representation:**
- 2 US Senators
- House Representatives (proportional to population)
- Electoral College votes
- Right to vote in presidential elections

### GAO Data on Statehood Benefits (from Puerto Rico analysis)

Of 29 major federal programs reviewed by the GAO (accounting for 86% of federal program spending for states), statehood would affect 11 programs -- all expanding benefits:
- Four largest affected: Medicare, Medicaid, SNAP, and SSI
- Estimated additional federal funding: $10 billion+ per year
- SSI benefits substantially higher than territory-level assistance
- Full bankruptcy protection for government agencies and municipalities

### Hawaii and Alaska Implementation (1959)

- Alaska admitted: January 3, 1959
- Hawaii admitted: August 21, 1959
- Federal spending and tourism prompted immediate economic boom in both states
- Full federal program access began upon admission
- Hawaii constitutional convention: 101 days total (78 working days), April 4-July 22, 1950 -- years before formal statehood
- Federal agencies were already operating in both territories; statehood elevated and expanded their role

## 4. The Texas Annexation Referendum (1845)

### The Vote

**Date:** October 13, 1845

**What was on the ballot:** Three questions:
1. Adoption or rejection of annexation to the United States
2. Adoption or rejection of the proposed state constitution
3. An ordinance concerning colony contracts

**Vote tallies (initial count):**
- Annexation: 4,245 FOR / 257 AGAINST
- State constitution: 4,174 FOR / 312 AGAINST

**Final compiled totals (January 1, 1846):**
- Annexation: 7,664 FOR / 430 AGAINST

**Percentage in favor:** approximately 94-95% voted YES for annexation

**How simple was it?** Three straightforward yes/no questions on a single ballot. Voters checked columns for/against each item on tally sheets.

**Context:** Texas had been an independent republic for 9 years (1836-1845). The vote was overwhelming -- almost no opposition. The people chose to join an existing system rather than continue building one from scratch.

**Timeline:** The entire process from US Congressional joint resolution (March 1, 1845) to voter approval (October 13, 1845) took approximately 7 months. Texas was formally admitted December 29, 1845.

## 5. State Constitution Process

### How Simple Is a State Constitution vs. a National One?

**Length comparison:**
- US Constitution: 4,543 words
- Average state constitution: ~26,000 words
- Shortest state constitution: Vermont, ~8,500 words
- Longest: Alabama, 310,000+ words (an outlier)
- California: 54,000+ words

State constitutions are longer because they are more detailed and policy-specific, but they are structurally simpler -- they don't need to invent a system of government from nothing. They operate within the existing federal framework.

### What Does a Typical State Constitution Contain?

- Bill of rights (often more expansive than federal)
- Executive branch structure (governor, lieutenant governor, attorney general)
- State legislature structure
- State court system including state supreme court
- Education system provisions
- Intrastate commerce regulation
- Local government structure
- State tax policies
- Marriage and family law provisions
- Amendment procedures

**What a state constitution does NOT need to cover** (because the federal government handles it):
- National defense and military
- Foreign policy and diplomacy
- Interstate commerce
- Currency and monetary policy
- Immigration
- International trade
- Federal criminal law
- Constitutional rights baseline (14th Amendment applies)

### Can It Be Written in Months?

**Historical examples:**
- Republic of Texas (1836): Drafted in approximately 2 weeks (March 2-17, 1836)
- California (1849): Constitutional convention lasted 6 weeks
- Hawaii (1950): Convention lasted 101 days (78 working days) -- roughly 3.5 months
- Texas 1845 state constitution: Drafted by convention and approved by voters in the same October 13, 1845 vote as annexation

**Answer: Yes.** Multiple state constitutions have been drafted in weeks to months. The federal framework eliminates the need to design an entire governmental system. A state constitution primarily organizes state-level governance within the existing federal structure.

### Contrast with Nation-Building Constitutions

| Metric | State Constitution | Iraq (National) | Libya (National) | Afghanistan (National) |
|--------|-------------------|-----------------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Time to draft | 2 weeks - 3.5 months | 2.5 years | 14+ years (still none) | 3 years |
| Survived? | Yes (all US state constitutions function) | Fragile, contested | Never adopted | Collapsed in 2021 |
| Required building military? | No (National Guard + federal) | Yes ($88B spent) | Yes (failed) | Yes ($88B, collapsed) |
| Required building courts? | State courts only (federal courts exist) | Entire judiciary from scratch | Still no functioning judiciary | Built and collapsed |
| Required building police? | State/local only (FBI exists) | 285,000 disbanded, rebuilt from scratch | Militia-controlled | Built and collapsed |
| Currency needed? | No (US dollar) | New dinar (volatile) | Dinar (unstable) | New afghani (aid-dependent) |
| Banking system needed? | No (FDIC, Federal Reserve) | Built from scratch | Still fragmented | Built and collapsed |

## Key Data Points Summary

- **Iraq:** 20+ years, $2+ trillion, 200,000+ dead, still fragile democracy
- **Libya:** 14+ years, still no constitution, failed state, rival governments
- **Egypt:** Revolution to military coup in 2 years 5 months, 2,000+ killed, back to authoritarianism
- **Afghanistan:** 20 years, $2.3 trillion, 200,000+ dead, total collapse in weeks
- **US Statehood:** Instant access to 100+ federal agencies, $10B+/year in federal funds, full constitutional rights, FDIC banking, Social Security, Medicare, federal courts, FBI, FEMA -- all on day one
- **State constitution:** Can be written in weeks to months (proven by California, Texas, Hawaii)
- **Texas referendum:** 95% voted yes on a simple ballot; 7 months from resolution to statehood
